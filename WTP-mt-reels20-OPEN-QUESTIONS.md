## Q2 — FCA compliance for #17 (UK Non-Dom): counsel sign-off OR geo-exclude UK?

**Context:** WTP2 is not FCA-regulated. Reel #17 is directed at UK residents (audience targeting + content explicitly about UK regime change). Under FCA FG24/1 ("Financial Promotions on Social Media", finalised March 2024), promotions reaching UK retail audiences must satisfy prominence requirements: risk warnings must be on the face of the promotion, ≥24pt-equivalent, and present for the full duration if the content discusses regulated activity.

**Where it gets messy:** Tax planning itself is outside FCA perimeter. But Reel #17 mentions "trusts, accounts, and holding companies all get re-examined" + "capital gains can crystallise on the move" — this can pull the content inside Section 21 FSMA (financial promotion) territory if interpreted as inducement to enter regulated arrangements (structured lending, trust investments).

**Primary sources:**
- FCA FG24/1 (Mar 2024): https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg24-1.pdf
- HMRC Apr 2025 non-dom changes: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/changes-to-the-taxation-of-non-uk-domiciled-individuals
- FSMA Section 21 perimeter guidance: https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/8/

**Recommended answer:** Option (a) — commission a UK partner counsel sign-off. Cost ~£500-1500 for a one-off review. Adds permanent disclaimer language WTP can reuse on any UK-touching content. UK partner firm on camera (Q1 option b) also reduces this risk because a UK-regulated person can carry the content.

**Decision options:**
- (a) Commission UK partner counsel sign-off (recommended)
- (b) Geo-exclude UK from this reel's targeting in Meta — instantly removes FCA jurisdiction question, costs UK audience reach
- (c) Hold reel #17 indefinitely until UK channel is built with proper compliance

**Effort:** Option (a) = 1-2 hr legal call + £500-1500. Option (b) = 5 min in Meta Ads. Option (c) = 0 + lose UK audience for now.

## Q4 — Cite Article 11 of FDL 41/2022 specifically, or stay generic?

**Context:** The "Article 241 of the UAE Personal Status Law" citation in original V1+V2 of Reel #8 was unverifiable. Primary source verification found that:
- Federal Decree-Law No. 41 of 2022 (effective 1 Feb 2023) governs non-Muslim Personal Status. Has 18 articles, not 241.
- Article 11 of that law is the operative non-Muslim succession provision (one-half to spouse, balance equally to children; if no children, parents).
- The "Waqf" framing in V1/V2 was also incorrect — assets do NOT default to Waqf for non-Muslims; they default to UAE Personal Status Law distribution which itself approximates Sharia portions in absence of registered Will.

**FINAL draft already removed the Article 241 + Waqf wording.** Current copy uses generic "UAE default inheritance rules can apply Sharia distribution to non-Muslim expat assets." This is correct but less authoritative-sounding.

**Recommended answer:** Add Article 11 citation back in if Olga signs off on the wording. Suggested replacement payload sentence: "Under Article 11 of Federal Decree-Law 41 of 2022, in the absence of a registered Will, half the estate goes to spouse and the remainder to children — your home-country distribution is not honoured."

**Primary sources:**
- Federal Decree-Law 41/2022 (Personal Status for Non-Muslims): https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1586
- Library of Congress summary: https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2023-03-13/united-arab-emirates-personal-status-law-for-non-muslims-enters-into-force/
