# The Second-RFI Readiness Checklist
*WTP · Banking-First series*

In a UAE corporate or private bank onboarding, the first Request for Information asks for documents. Most files clear it. The second cycle is different: it does not ask for more paper, it tests whether the paper you already filed holds together. This note explains what that second cycle probes and gives you a checklist to pre-answer it before you submit.

## What the second RFI actually tests

The first RFI confirms a file is *complete*. The second confirms it is *coherent*. Under FATF Recommendation 10, an institution is not finished once it has identified you; it must understand the purpose of the relationship and satisfy itself that activity is consistent with what it knows about you. The second cycle is where that standard is applied to your specific file. It typically probes four things.

- **Narrative-to-flow consistency.** Your stated source-of-funds story is read against the actual transaction pattern: counterparties, corridors, timing, amounts. FATF R.10 requires ongoing scrutiny to ensure transactions are consistent with the institution's knowledge of the customer. A clean narrative that does not match the flow is the most common second-cycle failure.
- **Source of wealth vs source of funds — two separate questions.** FATF treats these as distinct. *Source of wealth* is how your total estate was accumulated over a lifetime (the business you built, the assets you sold, the inheritance). *Source of funds* is the origin of the specific money in this transfer or this account. A file that answers one as if it answered the other will be sent back. In higher-risk and PEP files, the FATF interpretive note to R.10 (and R.12 for PEPs) expects both to be established.
- **Beneficial-owner cross-checks.** Where a structure is involved, the institution must identify the natural person who ultimately owns or controls it, and verify that identity rather than accept a declaration. A beneficial-owner interview tests whether the person named on the form is the person who actually controls the funds, and whether their account of the structure matches the documents.
- **Economic rationale of the structure.** A holding company, trust, or multi-jurisdiction layer is expected to have a coherent commercial or estate reason. The second cycle asks why the structure exists. "Tax efficiency" alone, with no operating substance or succession logic, reads as opacity rather than rationale.

In the UAE this is not discretionary bank policy. It flows from Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018 and its implementing Cabinet Decision No. 10 of 2019 (as amended), which require customer due diligence, understanding of the purpose and ownership structure of the relationship, and enhanced due diligence on higher-risk profiles. A connected reason the second cycle exists: under the OECD Common Reporting Standard, account information is exchanged automatically across participating jurisdictions, so an institution expects your filed narrative to be consistent with what it can independently see about your cross-border position.

## Pre-submission checklist

Self-apply before you file. If any item is "no," resolve it before submission, not after a second RFI.

- [ ] **One narrative, written down.** Your source-of-funds explanation exists as a single, dated, plain-language paragraph — not assembled differently each time it is asked.
- [ ] **Narrative reconciles to the flow.** Every material inflow you expect the bank to see maps to a line in your narrative. Unexplained large credits are identified and documented in advance.
- [ ] **Wealth and funds answered separately.** You can state, in two distinct paragraphs, (a) how the overall estate was built over time and (b) where the money in *this* transfer originated — and the two connect logically.
- [ ] **Each claim has a document behind it.** Sale of a company, property disposal, dividend, salary, inheritance — each named source is backed by a contract, statement, tax filing, or equivalent.
- [ ] **Beneficial owner is unambiguous.** For any company, trust, or nominee arrangement, the natural person who ultimately owns or controls it is named, and supporting evidence is ready before any interview.
- [ ] **The interviewee can tell the story unaided.** The beneficial owner can explain the structure and the funds in their own words, consistently with the filed documents.
- [ ] **The structure has a stated reason.** You can give a one-line economic or succession rationale for each layer, beyond tax.
- [ ] **Corridors and counterparties are explainable.** Any jurisdiction or counterparty likely to draw scrutiny is anticipated, with a documented reason it appears in your flow.
- [ ] **Dates and amounts are internally consistent.** Figures and timelines agree across the application form, the narrative, and the supporting documents.
- [ ] **No gaps you would have to explain live.** Anything a reviewer could question is pre-answered in the file, not left for a follow-up cycle.
