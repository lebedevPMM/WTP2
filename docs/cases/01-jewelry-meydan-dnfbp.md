## Client Brief

The client planned to launch a premium jewelry brand with a global business model:

1. **Source** gold and precious stones in UAE
2. **Export** raw materials to Armenia for manufacturing
3. **Produce** finished jewelry at a partner factory
4. **Import** finished products back to UAE
5. **Sell** globally via e-commerce
6. **Accept** cryptocurrency payments legally (BTC, ETH, USDT)

The project combined multiple regulatory challenges: DNFBP classification (highest compliance tier), international precious goods logistics, and crypto payment integration — all within a single company structure.

---

## Challenge

### Why this case was complex

**1. DNFBP Classification**
Gold and precious stones trading falls under DNFBP — Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions. This category requires:
- Pre-approval before license issuance
- Full AML/CFT compliance framework from day one
- Ongoing reporting obligations
- Enhanced due diligence requirements
- Most UAE banks apply additional scrutiny or decline DNFBP applications outright

**2. Multi-Activity License Architecture**
The business model required five distinct activities under a single license — each with different compliance implications. Incorrect activity selection would either limit operations or trigger unnecessary regulatory requirements.

**3. International Supply Chain**
Exporting precious goods from UAE, manufacturing abroad, and re-importing finished products requires:
- Licensed secure logistics operators
- Proper customs and trade documentation
- Compliance with AML requirements at every transfer point
- Correctly structured manufacturing agreements

**4. Crypto Payment Integration**
Accepting cryptocurrency in UAE without a VARA license requires specific structuring through licensed payment processors. The model must ensure the company receives fiat while customers pay in crypto.

**5. Banking**
A DNFBP company with an international production model and crypto exposure is among the most challenging profiles for UAE corporate banking. Estimated rejection rate at first attempt without proper preparation: ~80%.

## WTP Approach

### Step 1: Banking-First Assessment

Before any registration, we:
- Mapped the client's full business model and risk profile
- Identified 3 banks known to accept DNFBP profiles with this activity mix
- Pre-structured the business description to address compliance questions proactively
- Prepared Source of Funds documentation aligned with bank expectations
- Determined the optimal activity combination that satisfied business needs without triggering unnecessary red flags

**Decision:** Proceed — profile is bankable with proper preparation.

### Step 2: Jurisdiction Selection

Meydan Free Zone was selected based on:
- Support for DNFBP activities (gold, precious stones)
- Competitive cost structure for the activity mix
- Speed of processing for complex license applications
- Track record with jewelry and luxury goods businesses

### Step 3: License Architecture

Final activity configuration:
| # | Activity | Purpose |
|---|----------|---------|
| 1 | Wholesale of Gold & Other Precious Metals | Sourcing raw materials in UAE |
| 2 | Wholesale of Precious Stones | Sourcing gemstones |
| 3 | Jewellery Trading (retail & wholesale) | B2B and B2C sales |
| 4 | E-commerce / Online Trading | Global online sales channel |
| 5 | DNFBP Registration (pre-approval) | Regulatory requirement for gold/stones |

### Step 4: DNFBP Compliance

- Submitted full AML/CFT compliance framework for pre-approval
- Appointed MLRO (Money Laundering Reporting Officer)
- Documented internal policies: CDD, EDD, STR filing, record-keeping, training
- Passed DNFBP pre-approval review

### Step 5: SIRA Inspection

Security Industries Regulatory Agency (SIRA) inspection was mandatory:
- Physical security assessment
- Storage protocols for precious goods
- Operational security procedures
- Access control documentation

### Step 6: International Trade Structure

- Engaged licensed secure logistics operators for precious goods transport (UAE → Armenia)
- Structured manufacturing agreement with Armenian partner factory
- Documented re-import procedures for finished jewelry (Armenia → UAE)
- Verified compliance with international trade and AML requirements at each point

### Step 7: Crypto Payment Integration

Implemented legal crypto acceptance through licensed payment processors:
- **Model:** Customer pays in BTC/ETH/USDT → Processor converts to fiat → Company receives AED/USD
- **Providers evaluated:** BitPay, Binance Pay, Fuze
- **Regulatory status:** No VARA license required (company does not hold, trade, or custody crypto)
- **Compliance:** Fully aligned with UAE Central Bank regulations

### Step 8: Banking

- Submitted prepared application to pre-selected bank
- Provided structured business description addressing DNFBP compliance, international model, and crypto processing
- Supported the bank's due diligence process with pre-prepared documentation
- Account opened

## Key Takeaways

1. **Banking-first saved weeks.** Pre-assessing bankability before registration prevented wasted time on a structure that might not get a bank account.

2. **Activity mix matters.** The wrong combination of activities could have triggered additional regulatory requirements or limited the business model.

3. **DNFBP is not a blocker — it's a process.** With proper compliance preparation, DNFBP companies can open bank accounts and operate normally. The key is doing the compliance work upfront, not after registration.

4. **Crypto is legal in UAE — with the right structure.** Using licensed payment processors for crypto-to-fiat conversion keeps the company outside VARA's regulatory scope while enabling crypto acceptance.

5. **International structures need documentation.** Every point in the supply chain — export, manufacturing, import — must be documented and compliant. This isn't optional; it's what banks and regulators verify.
